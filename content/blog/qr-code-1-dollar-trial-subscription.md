If you paid $1 or so to download a QR code and were later charged a much bigger amount, you most likely started a short trial of a subscription, and it renewed at full price. On many QR sites the cheap first payment buys a 7-day trial, and the code only keeps working while you keep paying. Cancel on the company's own website now to stop the next charge, keep the confirmation email, then ask them in writing for a refund. Your printed code will probably stop working once the subscription ends, so plan for that too.

## What usually happened

The pattern people describe on Reddit, X and review sites goes like this. You make a QR code, the site says it's ready, and the download button leads to a checkout. The big number is $0.99 or $1. The smaller text says it's a trial that becomes a recurring plan after a few days.

GetQR is the example in the news this month, so here is how it describes its own billing. Its help centre says GetQR ["is a subscription-based service"](https://getqr.com/help/subscription-recurring-charge), that "an active subscription is required to keep your QR codes live", and that your plan "activates automatically at the end of the 7-day trial period". Its [renewal article](https://getqr.com/help/automatically-renew-subscription) lists the plans that renew on their own: trial, 4-week, quarterly and annual. Section 9 of its [terms](https://getqr.com/eula) sets the trial at $1.00 USD (CA$1.50 in Canada) for 7 days, then a 4-week subscription at $39.99 USD (CA$44.99) unless you cancel before the trial ends. The terms also say the trial fee is non-refundable.

GetQR isn't the only site that works this way, and the terms are on its pages. The problem is that people go in expecting to pay once for a file and come out with a subscription.

Two details catch people out:

- **"Every 4 weeks" is not monthly.** That's 13 charges a year, not 12. At $39.99 that's about $520 a year.
- **The code is dynamic.** The pattern holds the company's short link, not your website. Every scan goes through their server first, which is why the code can be switched off when the payments stop.

## Stop the next charge

1. **Cancel on the company's website.** If you paid on a website, the subscription isn't in your phone's app store settings. GetQR, for example, says you can cancel through its [Cancellation Portal or Account > Manage My Plan](https://getqr.com/help/cancel-my-subscription).
2. **Keep proof.** Screenshot the "cancelled" screen and keep the confirmation email. GetQR's own help says that if you have [no confirmation email](https://getqr.com/help/charge-after-cancel), the cancellation didn't go through.
3. **Check for a second account.** If you signed up with a different email address, you may have an active subscription you can't see when you log in with your usual one.
4. **Know that cancelling isn't a refund.** It stops future charges. A charge that already went through stays unless the company refunds it.

## Ask for your money back

Write to the company soon, from the email address on the account. Say plainly that you meant to buy one code, that you cancelled as soon as you saw the charge, and that you haven't used the paid plan. Ask for a refund of the renewal charge.

Read their refund policy first so you know what you're up against. GetQR's [refund policy](https://getqr.com/help/refund-policy) says payments are "generally final", lists duplicate or wrong charges and technical problems as cases it may refund, rules out change of mind and non-use, and asks for requests within 30 days of the charge.

If the company says no and you think the terms weren't shown clearly, you can ask your bank or card issuer to dispute the charge. They'll review it; a refund isn't guaranteed, and acting quickly helps. A dispute doesn't cancel the subscription, though. GetQR's terms say removing your card, a chargeback or contacting your bank "does not count as cancellation", so cancel on the site as well.

## What happens to the code you printed

Once the subscription ends, expect the code to stop opening your link. GetQR's own [troubleshooting article](https://getqr.com/help/qr-code-not-working) lists "your subscription or trial has expired/ended" as the first reason a code stops working, and paying again as the fix.

Paying a different QR company won't bring it back. The short link inside the pattern belongs to the company that made it, and only they can forward it. If the code is on something you can't reprint, your choices are:

- **Pay that company for as long as the printed item matters.** Sometimes this is the cheapest option for a single wedding invitation or event that's nearly over.
- **Cover it.** Print a new code on a sticker and put it over the old one.
- **Reprint.** For cards, flyers and menus, this is usually the cleanest fix.

## Common questions

### Why was I charged $39.99 after paying $1 for a QR code?

The $1 most likely paid for a short trial of a subscription. If you didn't cancel before the trial ended, the plan renewed at full price. GetQR's terms, for example, set a $1.00 USD 7-day trial that turns into $39.99 USD every 4 weeks unless you cancel.

### How do I stop GetQR from charging me again?

Cancel through GetQR's Cancellation Portal or Account > Manage My Plan, and keep the confirmation email. GetQR says no confirmation email means the cancellation didn't go through. Cancelling stops future charges but doesn't refund past ones.

### Will my QR code stop working if I cancel?

If it's a dynamic code from a subscription service, usually yes, once the paid period ends. GetQR says an active subscription is required to keep codes live. Static codes are different: they hold your link in the pattern and keep working.

### Can I move my printed QR code to another company?

No, unless the code points to a domain you own. The pattern holds the original company's short link, so only that company can forward it. The fix is a sticker over the old code or a reprint.

### Do free QR code generators charge you later?

Static generators that let you download without a card don't. Charges come from sites that ask for a card before the download, usually for a trial that renews. If a site asks for a card to download one QR code, read the terms beside the price first.
