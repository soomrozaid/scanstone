Most complaints about QR code menus aren't about the QR code. They're about what happens after the scan: a menu that won't load on one bar of signal, a cookie banner before the prices, a sign-up or phone number prompt, a PDF you have to pinch and zoom, or no paper menu when a phone dies. Each of those has a plain fix. Keep paper menus for anyone who asks, link the code straight to a fast public menu page or a small file, print the menu address under the code, and test it from the worst seat in the room on mobile data. Then the QR code is a shortcut, not the only way to order.

## People really are tired of bad QR menus

This isn't just a loud corner of social media. Ipsos has asked Americans about QR code menus since 2022. In its July 2025 poll, 65% said they had used one. Among those people, 58% said they would like to go back to paper menus (48% in 2022), 71% said they were glad some restaurants had gone back to paper, and only 37% said they liked the experience of using digital menus.

Posts on X this month say the same thing in plainer words: a diner who walked out because the QR-only menu didn't work and staff couldn't take the order by voice, "I came here to eat, not to load a website on 1 bar of signal", and "the restaurant's printing cost quietly moves to the customer: your phone, battery, data, and patience".

So if you use a QR menu, the bar is high. The good news is that most of the problems are in your control.

## Make the menu fast on bad signal

The scan takes a second. The menu loading is what people remember. A menu exported straight from design software for printing can be several megabytes because of full-size photos. On a weak signal in a basement or a patio, that's a long wait.

- **Export for screens, not print.** Most design and PDF tools have a "smallest file size" or "web" export. Resize photos before you add them.
- **Or skip the photos.** A text menu with prices loads fast everywhere. A single compressed image of each page is also fine, and some guests prefer it. One person on X put it simply: a QR menu should be a photo of the menu.
- **Keep it at one address.** If you replace the file at the same link when prices change, the printed code keeps working without any change.
- **Test it the way guests will.** Turn off WiFi, stand at the table farthest from the door, scan, and time how long until you can read a price.

## Put nothing between the scan and the menu

Every step between the scan and the menu loses people: cookie banners, newsletter pop-ups, "download our app", location requests, a login. If your menu lives on your website and the site shows a cookie banner, link the code to the menu file directly instead.

The QR code itself can add a step too. Some QR services send scans through their own page first, or show ads, or a "this code is deactivated" page after a trial. Scan your code and watch what opens. It should go straight to your menu.

## Keep paper menus and say so

The fix for the guest whose phone is dead, who doesn't own a smartphone, or who just prefers paper is a paper menu. You don't need a full laminated reprint every time prices change. A small stack of plain printed copies at the host stand, dated so you know when to replace them, covers it.

Put one line on the table card next to the code: "Prefer a paper menu? Just ask." Then it's a choice, not a test. Large-print copies help guests who find small type hard to read on a phone or on paper.

## Print the code so it scans first time

- **Size:** big enough to scan from where people sit. If guests have to pick up the card and lean in, it's too small.
- **Contrast:** dark code on a light background. Don't put it on a busy photo.
- **Margin:** leave the white border around the code. Cropping it is a common reason codes won't scan.
- **Surface:** matte card or paper. Glossy lamination under restaurant lights reflects into the camera.
- **Placement:** flat or upright, not across a fold or on a curved bottle.
- **The address:** print the menu address in plain text under the code, like yourcafe.com/menu, for anyone whose camera won't cooperate.
- **File:** use an SVG for print so it stays sharp at any size.

## Don't put a trial code on your tables

A menu code that stops working is worse than no code. Several QR companies switch dynamic codes off when a free trial ends. Two restaurant owners on Reddit described exactly that: their menu codes died after 14-day trials with no warning, one right after a full reprint.

If your menu stays at one address, you don't need a dynamic code. A static code to that address has nothing to expire. If you want to change the address later or count scans, use a dynamic code from a company that says in writing what happens when you stop paying. ## A two-minute check before service

1. Scan the code on the table with WiFi off.
2. Count the seconds until you can read a price.
3. Note anything that appeared before the menu: banner, pop-up, login, app prompt.
4. Check prices and sold-out items match today's menu.
5. Ask a host where the paper menus are.

If any step fails, fix that one thing. If a code stops opening at all, our guide to [a QR code that stopped working](/blog/qr-code-stopped-working-how-to-fix) goes through the causes.

## Common questions

### Why do people hate QR code menus?

Mostly because of what happens after the scan: slow pages on weak signal, cookie banners and pop-ups, logins or app prompts, menus that are hard to read on a phone, and no paper option. In Ipsos's July 2025 poll, 58% of people who had used a QR menu said they would like to go back to paper menus.

### Should restaurants still use QR code menus?

A QR code works well as an extra, next to paper menus, not as the only way to see the menu. Keep paper copies, link the code to a fast public menu, and keep taking orders at the table.

### Is a PDF menu OK for a QR code?

Yes, if it's small and readable. Export it for screens rather than print so it loads on weak signal, and use real text rather than a photo of a printed menu. A plain menu page is easier to read on a phone, but a light PDF or image is better than a heavy website with pop-ups.

### How do I make my QR menu load faster?

Make the file smaller (resize photos, export for web), link straight to the menu instead of a page with banners and widgets, and test on mobile data from the far end of the room.

### Do guests need an app to scan a menu QR code?

No. Most phone cameras read QR codes and open the link in the browser. Your menu page shouldn't ask for an app or an account either.

### Will my menu QR code stop working?

A static code keeps working as long as the menu address inside it does. A dynamic code depends on the QR company: some switch codes off when a trial ends or you stop paying.
