If QR Code Creator (qrcodecreator.com) charged you $133.20 when you expected $11.10, you paid for a year of its Basic plan. Its pricing page shows Basic as "$11.1 /month" with "Billed annually" underneath, so the first charge is 12 months up front: $11.10 x 12 = $133.20. Turn off auto-renew now so it doesn't charge you again next year, then ask for a refund in writing. Your code keeps working until the paid year ends, which gives you time to replace it with one that doesn't need a subscription.

## Why the charge was $133.20

QR Code Creator's [pricing page](https://qrcodecreator.com/pricing) (checked November 26, 2026) lists three plans, each shown as a monthly price that is billed once a year, in USD with tax not included:

| Plan | Price shown | Charged up front for the year | Dynamic codes | Scans |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | $11.1 / month | $133.20 | 3 | 20,000 |
| Advanced | $15.58 / month | $186.96 | 50 | Unlimited |
| Professional | $46 / month | $552.00 | 250 | Unlimited |

The yearly totals are our arithmetic from the monthly prices. The page doesn't show a month-to-month option.

The usual path to this charge looks like this. You make a "free" code, and your account starts a 7-day free trial when you register. The disclaimer on the [QR Code Creator home page](https://qrcodecreator.com/) says that after the trial, "Dynamic QR codes generated during the trial will be temporarily deactivated", while static codes keep working. Section 13.4 of its [terms](https://qrcodecreator.com/terms) adds that deactivated codes "lead to the service page" until you buy a plan. So a code that is already printed on invitations, posters or business cards stops opening your link, emails arrive saying it is expiring, and the cheapest way to switch it back on looks like $11.10.

People who were caught by this describe the same thing in reports on the [BBB Scam Tracker](https://www.bbb.org/scamtracker/lookupscam/1170814), several of them from 2025 and 2026: they picked the $11.10 option, read it as a monthly charge, and saw $133.20 leave their account. Those are customer accounts, not findings, but they match the pricing page.

## Stop the next charge

The $133.20 already went through, but the plan renews on its own each year. Turn that off first.

1. Sign in and go to **My Account**, then **Billing**, then **Payment Details**.
2. Click **Manage** and choose **Disable Auto-Renew**.
3. Enter your password, tick "I want to cancel my subscription", and finish the short survey.
4. Keep the confirmation screen or email.

Those are the steps from QR Code Creator's own help article, ["Can I cancel my subscription?"](https://help.qrcodecreator.com/en/articles/10775194-can-i-cancel-my-subscription). Section 18.1 of the terms says cancelling "will take effect at the end of your current paid term", so turning off auto-renew doesn't switch your code off today.

Section 13.5 of the terms also promises an email "no later than thirty (30) calendar days" before an annual plan renews, sent from support@qrcodecreatormail.com. If you keep the plan for now, watch for that email next year.

## Ask for a refund

Be realistic about what the terms say. Section 18.1: "All purchases of the Services are final, and no refund shall be provided due to cancellation except as specifically noted in these Terms." Its help article on [refunds after cancelling](https://help.qrcodecreator.com/en/articles/10394557-if-i-cancel-my-subscription-will-i-get-a-refund) (October 19, 2026) says there are no refunds for the remaining time on your billing period.

The terms do name two exceptions:

- **EU and UK residents** have a 14-day right of withdrawal (section 18.2). The terms say this right can end early once you agree to start using the service and download or stream content, so it may not apply if you've already used the plan.
- **Access failure** (section 18.3). If you paid and then couldn't get into your account, the code didn't work, or the dashboard didn't work, you must email them within 24 hours of paying with your email address, customer ID, invoice number, payment date and amount. If they can't fix it within 48 hours, you get a full refund.

Outside those cases, it is still worth writing. Send one short email from the address on the account to support@qrcodecreator.com: say you chose the plan shown as $11.10 a month, you didn't see that you would be charged $133.20 for a year, you've turned off auto-renew, and you're asking for the charge to be refunded. Keep a copy. The company behind the site is Convertizing LTD in Dublin, Ireland, as stated in its terms.

If they say no and you believe the yearly total wasn't made clear before you paid, you can ask your bank or card issuer about disputing the charge. They decide, and a refund isn't guaranteed. If the refund or dispute succeeds, expect the paid plan to end and the code to go back to the service page, so have a replacement ready first.

## What to do about the code you printed

The code holds a short link that belongs to QR Code Creator, so only QR Code Creator can keep it working. Another QR company can't take it over. While your paid year runs, the code works. Use that time:

- **If the link won't change, switch to a static code.** A static code holds your web address in the pattern itself, so there is nothing to switch off. Any free static generator will do; ours is at [/qr-code-generator](/qr-code-generator), with free PNG and SVG downloads, no account and no card.
- **Replace the printed code before the year ends.** For cards, flyers and menus, reprint. For signs or items you can't reprint, a sticker with the new code over the old one works.
- **If the event is over, you may not need to do anything.** A baby shower invite or a one-off poster can simply let the code lapse when the year ends.

## How to avoid it next time

- **Scan before you print.** If the address that comes up is your own website, the code is static. If it's a short link on the QR company's domain, it depends on them.
- **Look under the price.** "Billed annually" under a monthly figure means the whole year is charged today.
- **Be wary of a "free" code that needs a sign-up.** That often means a trial of a dynamic code, and the code stops when the trial does.
